About

Environmental scientist and engineer, working on the measurement of airborne particulate matter.

My work is on making inexpensive optical sensors accurate enough that the data they produce can be relied upon.

I completed my Ph.D. in the Environmental Science and Engineering Department at IIT Bombay in May 2026, supervised by Prof. Manoranjan Sahu. My thesis, Calibration of Low-cost Particulate Matter Sensors Using Physics-based and Machine Learning Frameworks: From Laboratory Evaluation to Field Applications, ran from controlled chamber experiments through to multi-site deployments across Delhi–NCR and the Indo-Gangetic Plain. The work was supported by the Prime Minister's Research Fellowship.

Before that I completed an M.Sc. in the same department, working on the techno-economics of particulate control technologies — a review from that period became a Springer book chapter. My undergraduate degree is in Physical Sciences from Kirori Mal College, University of Delhi, and the physics I learned there is still what I reach for first when a sensor behaves strangely.

I now work at Ubiqedge Technologies as Technical Expert for Air Quality Sensor Technologies, leading network-scale calibration and source characterisation for a low-cost sensor deployment across construction sites in Mumbai. I am looking toward a research career in academia.

Some of this started early. At seventeen I placed fourth at the Intel International Science and Engineering Fair for a paper-based diagnostic sensor for multiple sclerosis, after earlier projects on antimicrobial resistance and keratin waste valorisation. Different subjects, same instinct: build the affordable instrument, then prove it works.
