Research

Five threads, one question: when can you believe a cheap sensor?

Calibration chambers

Sensor evaluation studies rarely agree with one another, largely because they are run under conditions nobody else can reproduce. I designed and commissioned a 1.3 m³ environmental chamber at IIT Bombay with independent control over aerosol source, concentration, temperature and relative humidity, and used it to establish a protocol for assessing low-cost PM sensors against reference instrumentation. The resulting two-part study in ACS ES&T Air treats number and mass concentration separately, because a sensor that performs acceptably on one can fail badly on the other — a distinction that matters when the same device is used for both exposure work and regulatory comparison.

Physical-optical calibration

Most sensor corrections are empirical: fit against a reference, apply the fit, hope the conditions hold. They usually do not. My current work builds the correction from the instrument outward instead — modelling Mie scattering through the sensor's own optical geometry and detector response, and treating relative humidity through κ–Köhler particle growth rather than as a regression variable. The advantage is transferability: a model grounded in the physics of the measurement does not need refitting every time the aerosol changes. This manuscript is currently under review.

Sensor networks and spatial transferability

A city-scale network cannot afford a reference monitor beside every node, so calibrations must travel. I have examined how far they travel and why they stop — across a Delhi–NCR network, and using MERRA-2 reconstructed PM2.5 as a calibration proxy where no reference instrument was available at all. Alongside this I designed and implemented the IoT-based sensor network architecture across the IIT Bombay campus, including the calibration algorithms embedded in the platform software.

Source apportionment

Calibration and source attribution are the same problem viewed from opposite ends: both depend on what the particles actually are. I have worked on receptor modelling from low-cost sensor data in urban environments, and on machine-learning classification to label the factors that factor-analysis receptor models return — a step that is usually done by hand and inconsistently.

Measurement for policy

I built the end-to-end analysis pipeline for a pilot study assessing a large outdoor air-cleaning system — a “smog tower” — at Anand Vihar and Connaught Place in Delhi, integrating reference monitors with sensor arrays to test a heavily publicised intervention against measurement. I have also contributed to a T20 policy brief for the G20 on strengthening air quality monitoring through low-cost sensor and satellite synergies.
