Research interests

My work sits where aerosol physics, sensing hardware and data science meet, and it is aimed at one practical end: particulate matter measurements that people can act on.

  • Sensor characterisation and validation in controlled environments
  • Machine learning for sensor calibration
  • Meteorological data integration and prediction
  • Aerosol source apportionment and factor analysis
  • Urban IoT air quality monitoring networks
  • Spatiotemporal analysis of particulate matter, with an India focus
  • Emission control technologies, industrial and indoor
  • Human exposure and health impact assessment

From sparse networks to reliable PM2.5 mass estimates

My doctoral research presents an integrated calibration framework for low-cost particulate matter sensors, assimilating data from field deployments, controlled laboratory environments, physical models, and satellite-derived proxies into a unified computational engine. Key contributions include:

  • Spatial Transferability: Engineered a novel framework to quantify and evaluate the spatial transferability of empirical calibration models across distributed, high-density urban sensor networks.
  • Chamber-Based Characterization: Conducted systematic environmental chamber evaluations to isolate and correlate LCS particle number and mass-scattering responses with independently controlled physicochemical aerosol properties and meteorological parameters.
  • Instrument-Specific Bias Elucidation: Quantified differential measurement biases between the PMS5003 and OPC-N3 architectures when exposed to identical aerosol populations, attributing performance disparities to distinct optical geometries, size binning configurations, and proprietary internal mass-conversion algorithms.
  • Physics-Informed Modeling: Formulated a first-principles theoretical pathway to simulate LCS response dynamics by coupling Mie scattering theory, κ–Köhler thermodynamic growth, and sensor-specific optical parameters, establishing a foundation for autonomous, physics-informed self-calibration.
